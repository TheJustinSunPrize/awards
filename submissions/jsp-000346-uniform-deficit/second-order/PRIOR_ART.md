# Sources and novelty boundary

1. Kyle Pratt, *A “Digested” Proof of Erdős Problem #421*, [author's exposition](https://www.erdosproblems.com/static/421-Pratt.pdf), inspected PDF SHA-256 `7d3852fd9b0eeb2ab9dbecbe9cf2a145b89c060f2e491d8064a823a49d55f8ba`. Proposition 7.1, printed pp. 17–18, gives unconditional coefficient `1/3` and density-one coefficient `1` at square-root order. Section 7 asks whether an optimal square-root-order omission construction exists. The new second-order obstruction does not answer that construction question. The original existence proof and its attribution to Chojecki, Sneiderman, Sharma and predecessors remain separate.
2. Parent [PR #470](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/pull/470), initial proof commit `751ec8d4f50a620ff5eafa37033dc17bbd8882d3`: unconditional coefficient one with fourth-root finite error. The present proof develops its disjoint-omission counting idea by adding a second block length. This is a continuation of that contribution.
3. [PR #457](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/pull/457), `KunHcz`, updated proof commit `b5a18b882e75f8e54ff045f06b4525e8fc2e0855`, inspected on 2026-09-17. The updated statement gives `floor(sqrt(N))≤E(N)+max(L−1,0)` when `floor(sqrt(N))<(L+1)!`, hence a logarithmic corollary, without density assumptions. Its documentation cites #470's earlier coefficient-one bound. We explicitly recognize #457's stronger *unconditional error* result. Its minimal-crossing proof and code are not reused here.
4. [Erdős 421 statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/40e7c98697de6f66b8cbdbf641749ab39ed9c152/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/421.lean): the quantified closed-interval product condition agrees with the original problem. The present module defines the same mathematical condition directly; no source from that repository is imported or assumed as a theorem.

The distinguishing result here is a **positive cube-root second-order term under natural density one**, supported by a density-free two-scale finite inequality. The previous claims of an unconditional coefficient-one bound are not presented as new in this update. The density-one assumption cannot be silently dropped when comparing this result with #457.
