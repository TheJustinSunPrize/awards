# Source and prior-formalization audit

Audit date: 2026-09-17 (Asia/Shanghai). This records the sources inspected for
JSP-000746, not an assertion of priority over every unpublished formalization.

## Problem identity and mathematical attribution

**JSP-000746 corresponds to Erdős Problem #895.** The prize catalog asks whether
every sufficiently large triangle-free graph on an integer interval contains
three independent vertices of the form `a`, `b`, `a + b`:

- [Prize catalog entry](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/blob/f4e7173d89dfe91022a185427d63452c8ffbf6ae/problems/catalog-0701-0800.md#JSP-000746).
- [Erdős Problem #895](https://www.erdosproblems.com/895).
- [Official problem discussion page, including the complete problem statement and attribution](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/895).

The official discussion page was accessible during this audit. It credits the
question to Erdős and Hajnal and the explicit result for every `n ≥ 18` to
**Ben Barber, by SAT verification reported through personal communication**.
It cites the original problem source as `[Er95d]`. The page does not give a
publication date for Barber's communication; no date such as “2015” is asserted
here solely on the basis of a later software repository's comments.

The formalization target uses positive integers, ordinary natural-number
addition, and **three distinct vertices**. In particular `a = b` is excluded.
The statement for `[1,18]` corresponds to positive-valued vertices of `Fin 19`,
not `Fin 18`; the zero vertex is irrelevant. A graph on a larger interval
restricts to `[1,18]`, so a proof at that bound answers the stated sufficiently
large interval question.

This work formalizes an already known mathematical result. Attribution to
Barber concerns the explicit SAT bound recorded by the problem website, not
an assertion that qualitative existence was first established by Barber.

## Earlier qualitative literature

The following earlier paper is independently confirmed by
[Rödl's own publication list, under 1998](https://math.emory.edu/~rodl/publications.html)
and the publisher/Crossref bibliographic record:

Tomasz Łuczak, Vojtěch Rödl, and Tomasz Schoen,
*Independent finite sums in graphs defined on the natural numbers*,
Discrete Mathematics **181** (1998), 289–294,
[DOI 10.1016/S0012-365X(97)00064-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(97)00064-2).

The subsequent primary research paper by David S. Gunderson, Imre Leader,
Hans Jürgen Prömel, and Vojtěch Rödl,
[*Independent Deuber sets in graphs on the natural numbers*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0097316503001006),
Journal of Combinatorial Theory A **103** (2003), 305–322, Section 2,
explicitly attributes a positive answer to the independent Schur triple
question to that 1998 paper. It describes the stronger result for any fixed
finite number of distinct generators in a clique-free graph on the positive
integers. This supports the earlier qualitative attribution without supplying
the particular numerical bound 18.

The present submission does **not** formalize those general theorems and does
not claim an independent full finite-sums set generated by an infinite
sequence. Finite and infinite versions must not be conflated. The problem
website's ancillary Hindman-set status is not used as a mathematical premise.

## Existing Lean source: lower bound completed, positive theorem incomplete

The public repository `rjwalters/lean-genius` was inspected at the fixed commit
[`dc62f771ed5010abfdb04247dff6143e0e69d3e7`](https://github.com/rjwalters/lean-genius/tree/dc62f771ed5010abfdb04247dff6143e0e69d3e7),
obtained from GitHub's commits API on the audit date. Its commit timestamp is
2026-09-09T02:30:28Z.

- [Erdos895CounterexampleFin18.lean](https://github.com/rjwalters/lean-genius/blob/dc62f771ed5010abfdb04247dff6143e0e69d3e7/proofs/Proofs/Erdos895CounterexampleFin18.lean)
  provides a genuine simple graph on `Fin 18`, with vertex zero isolated,
  a 42-edge list on `[1,17]`, and kernel `decide` proofs of triangle-freeness
  and absence of an independent additive triple in distinct positive vertices.
  This is a lower-bound witness, not the universal positive theorem.
- [Erdos895Problem.lean, lines 143–146](https://github.com/rjwalters/lean-genius/blob/dc62f771ed5010abfdb04247dff6143e0e69d3e7/proofs/Proofs/Erdos895Problem.lean#L143)
  still has `sorry` in `barber_theorem`. The subsequently named `erdos_895`
  theorem invokes that incomplete theorem. Its original `IsAdditiveTriple`
  definition omits distinctness, although a corrected predicate is also present.
- [The repository's own correctness investigation](https://github.com/rjwalters/lean-genius/blob/dc62f771ed5010abfdb04247dff6143e0e69d3e7/research/problems/erdos-895-incomplete-01/knowledge.md)
  explicitly distinguishes the completed corrected counterexample from the
  unfinished positive direction and documents the indexing and distinctness
  pitfalls.

These are source-inspection findings; this audit did not rebuild that external
repository. A “complete” gallery badge for its counterexample does not certify
the positive theorem. The material inspected therefore does not establish an
existing complete Lean proof of the strict positive statement being submitted
here. It does establish prior Lean work on the same problem, which should be
acknowledged rather than described as absent.
