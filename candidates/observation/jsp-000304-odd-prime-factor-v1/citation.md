# Candidate observation

## English

This candidate observation is associated with [JSP-000304 — How large a prime factor must the product of two consecutive positive integers have?](https://github.com/TheJustinSunPrize/awards/blob/main/problems/catalog-0301-0400.md#JSP-000304). It publishes `Statement.lean` and `Proof.lean` for a complete, machine-checked Lean formalization of the answer to the catalog question: for every `n ≥ 2` the product `n * (n + 1)` of two consecutive positive integers always has a prime factor at least `3`, and the guaranteed bound `3` is optimal, being attained at `n = 8`, where `8 * 9 = 72 = 2³ · 3²` has largest prime factor exactly `3`. The theorems proved are `jsp000304 (n : ℕ) (hn : 2 ≤ n) : ∃ p : ℕ, Nat.Prime p ∧ 3 ≤ p ∧ p ∣ n * (n + 1)`, `prime_dvd_72_le_three : Nat.Prime p → p ∣ 72 → p ≤ 3`, `jsp000304_tight : ∃ n : ℕ, 2 ≤ n ∧ ∀ p, Nat.Prime p → p ∣ n * (n + 1) → p ≤ 3` (witness `n = 8`), and the combined form `jsp000304_complete : (∀ n : ℕ, 2 ≤ n → ∃ p : ℕ, Nat.Prime p ∧ 3 ≤ p ∧ p ∣ n * (n + 1)) ∧ ∃ n : ℕ, 2 ≤ n ∧ ∀ p, Nat.Prime p → p ∣ n * (n + 1) → p ≤ 3`. The argument is elementary: of two consecutive integers one is odd and, since `n ≥ 2`, that odd one is at least `3`; every prime factor of an odd number is an odd prime, hence at least `3`, and it divides the product. Tightness follows because every prime factor of `72 = 8 * 9 = 2³ · 3²` is at most `3`.

**Scope boundary:** this observation records the complete answer to the catalog question as stated — the guaranteed lower bound `3` and its optimality in the range `n ≥ 2`.
