# JSP-000116 Solution

## Problem Reference

JSP-000116

## Infinite Family of Minimum-Diameter Configurations

## Construction

Consider a configuration of four points:

$$
X=\{A,B,C,P\}
$$

Let \(A,B,C\) form an equilateral triangle with side length:

$$
|AB|=|BC|=|CA|=1
$$

The fourth point \(P\) is chosen such that:

$$
|PA|\leq 1
$$

$$
|PB|\leq 1
$$

$$
|PC|\leq 1
$$

The diameter of the configuration is defined by:

$$
D(X)=\max_{U,V\in X}|UV|
$$


## Minimum Diameter

Because the fixed triangle contains three unit-length edges:

$$
|AB|=|BC|=|CA|=1
$$

we immediately obtain the lower bound:

$$
D(X)\geq 1
$$

On the other hand, the construction requires every distance involving \(P\) to be at most \(1\), while all distances among \(A,B,C\) are exactly \(1\).

Therefore:

$$
D(X)\leq 1
$$

Combining both inequalities gives:

$$
D(X)=1
$$

Hence the minimum possible diameter is achieved.


## Infinite Family of Solutions

The above construction does not determine a unique position of \(P\).

Choose coordinates:

$$
A=(0,0)
$$

$$
B=(1,0)
$$

$$
C=(\frac12,\frac{\sqrt3}{2})
$$

The centroid of the equilateral triangle is:

$$
G=(\frac12,\frac{\sqrt3}{6})
$$

For this point:

$$
GA=GB=GC=\frac{\sqrt3}{3}<1
$$

Therefore \(G\) lies strictly inside the feasible region:

$$
B(A,1)\cap B(B,1)\cap B(C,1)
$$

Since this point is an interior point, there exists a positive number \(\epsilon\) such that every point

$$
P(t)=G+(t,0),\quad |t|<\epsilon
$$

remains feasible.

Thus every configuration

$$
X(t)=\{A,B,C,P(t)\}
$$

satisfies:

$$
D(X(t))=1
$$

Therefore there exists a continuous family of minimum-diameter configurations.


## Noncongruence Proof

It remains to show that this family contains infinitely many different configurations up to Euclidean congruence.

Consider the distance from the moving point \(P(t)\) to \(A\):

$$
|P(t)A|^2
=
(\frac12+t)^2+\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{6}\right)^2
$$

Expanding:

$$
|P(t)A|^2=t^2+t+\frac13
$$

This expression is not constant as \(t\) varies.

Therefore, for two different parameters:

$$
t_1\neq t_2
$$

chosen in the feasible interval, we have:

$$
|P(t_1)A|\neq |P(t_2)A|
$$

Euclidean congruence preserves all pairwise distances.

Hence:

$$
X(t_1)\not\cong X(t_2)
$$

whenever:

$$
t_1\neq t_2
$$

Therefore the family:

$$
\{X(t):|t|<\epsilon\}
$$

contains infinitely many pairwise noncongruent configurations.


## Conclusion

We have constructed an infinite one-parameter family of configurations satisfying the constraints and achieving:

$$
D(X)=1
$$

The configurations are pairwise noncongruent because their distance invariants vary continuously.

Hence the JSP-000116 problem admits infinitely many minimum-diameter configurations.
